12:Td43,<h1>Bengali – a brief pronunciation guide</h1>
<p>For a more extensive description, please see the appendix of the Maṭh’s <em>Śaraṇāgatī</em> publication. However, it is always best to hear from a native Bengali.</p>
<p><strong>Particularly note the following:</strong></p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Letter</th>
<th>Pronunciation</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody><tr>
<td><strong>a</strong></td>
<td>o in cot, but like the u in sum or the o in go when it occurs after a conjunct consonant or in the second syllable of a word; a is often silent when it follows a single consonant at the end of a word.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>ā</strong></td>
<td>a in father or star.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>ṛ</strong></td>
<td>ri in rim.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>e</strong></td>
<td>ay in play or the a in bat.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>ai</strong></td>
<td>o in cot combined with the ee in see.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>o</strong></td>
<td>o in go.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>au</strong></td>
<td>o in go combined with the oo in moon.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>ṅ</strong></td>
<td>ng in sing.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>ch</strong></td>
<td>ch in chop.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>chh</strong></td>
<td>ch pronounced with aspiration.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>ñ</strong></td>
<td>n in lunch.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>ṭ</strong></td>
<td>t in taught (pronounced cerebrally).</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>ṭh</strong></td>
<td>ṭ pronounced with aspiration. Never like the th in through.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>ḍ</strong></td>
<td>In between the d in dawn and the r in raw (pronounced cerebrally).</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>ḍh</strong></td>
<td>ḍ pronounced with aspiration.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>th</strong></td>
<td>t pronounced with aspiration. Never like the th in through.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>ph</strong></td>
<td>p pronounced with aspiration. Often sounds like the f in fall.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>b (v)</strong></td>
<td>b in ball. b if following a consonant, is silent; but preceding, is doubled.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>y</strong></td>
<td>When y is the first letter of a word, it is pronounced like the j in job. When y occurs elsewhere within a word and is preceded by a vowel, it is pronounced like the y in yawn or the w in wand. When y follows a consonant, it is silent and the preceding consonant is doubled, except when it is followed by r, in which case it is pronounced like the j in job.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>ś</strong>, <strong>ṣ</strong></td>
<td>sh in shawl.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>s</strong></td>
<td>s in song or the sh in shawl.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>ṁ</strong></td>
<td>Like a softened form of the ng in sing.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>ḥ</strong></td>
<td>ḥ is pronounced similar to a double h when occurring at the end of a word. When it occurs in the middle of a word, the consonant which follows it is doubled.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>( ̐)</strong></td>
<td>The chandrabindu indicates to nazalise the vowel beneath it.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>kṣ</strong></td>
<td>Pronounced like kh when at the beginning of a word and like k-kh when in the middle of a word.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>jñ</strong></td>
<td>Pronounced like g when at the beginning of a word and like g-g between vowels. An ā following jñ is pronounced like the a in bat.</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>
